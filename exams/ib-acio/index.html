Exam Pattern

Tier I is Computer-Based/Objective; Tier II is a descriptive (pen-and-paper or typed) written test; Tier III is an Interview. This entry covers Tier I and Tier II, the two written stages. · Tier I: 1 hour (60 minutes). Tier II: 1 hour (60 minutes).
Tier I (single paper)100 questions, 100 marks — General Awareness, Quantitative Aptitude, Reasoning, English, General Studies (combined into one objective paper, not separately sectioned/timed)
Tier II: Essay20 marks, descriptive
Tier II: English Comprehension10 marks, descriptive
Tier II: Two long-answer questions20 marks combined, descriptive
Negative MarkingTier I: -0.25 (1/4 mark) per wrong answer. Tier II: no negative marking (descriptive paper).
Passing MarksN/A — merit-based ranking, no fixed qualifying %. Tier I is used to shortlist for Tier II; Tier II in turn qualifies candidates for the Interview (Tier III), each stage typically has a category-wise cutoff decided after results, not a fixed universal percentage.

Secondary-sourced from multiple coaching-site aggregators, which agree on the Tier I (100Q/100 marks, -0.25) and Tier II (50-mark descriptive: Essay 20 + Comprehension 10 + two long-answers 20) structure; official notification is issued by MHA (mha.gov.in), which was not directly fetched. Frequently Asked Questions

Is IB ACIO's fee waiver the same as SSC's for women/SC/ST candidates?

Not quite — SSC exams typically waive the entire application fee for women, SC, ST, and PwBD candidates. IB ACIO only waives the Rs 100 "examination fee" portion; a separate Rs 550 "recruitment/processing charge" is payable by everyone, including exempted categories.

What kind of writing does the Tier II descriptive paper actually test?

It's a 50-mark paper combining a 20-mark essay, a 10-mark English comprehension passage, and two 20-mark long-answer questions — essentially testing your ability to write coherently and comprehend English at a level suited to an intelligence-analyst role.

Since Tier II has no negative marking, should I attempt every question?

Tier II is descriptive, so there's no "wrong answer" penalty the way there is in Tier I's objective format — but leaving sections blank still costs you all the marks for that section, so attempting every part (even partially) is generally advisable.

Does scoring well in Tier I guarantee a good final rank?

No — Tier I is only a screening/shortlisting stage for Tier II; your final merit combines Tier II (descriptive) and Interview performance, not your Tier I score, so a strong Tier I score alone doesn't secure the post.
