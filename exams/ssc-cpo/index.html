Posts & Pay

Level 6 · ₹35,400 – ₹1,12,400 (basic, 7th CPC Pay Matrix)Sub-Inspector (Executive), male and female — Delhi Police (classified Group C by Delhi Police); Sub-Inspector (GD) — Central Armed Police Forces (CRPF, BSF, ITBP, CISF, SSB) (Group B, Non-Gazetted, Non-Ministerial). One common notification and exam covers both; candidates give ranked preferences between Delhi Police and the CAPFs.

Confirmed from the official Notice, Paras 1.1–1.2: both Delhi Police SI (Executive) and CAPF SI (GD) sit at the same Level-6 pay band despite different Group classifications (C vs B). Male candidates for Delhi Police SI additionally need a valid LMV (Motorcycle & Car) driving licence to be eligible for Delhi Police at all — without one, male candidates can only be considered for CAPF SI posts (Notice, Para 7.7).

Eligibility

Age
  • This cycle: 20–25 years, as on 01 Aug 2025 (born between 02 Aug 2000 and 01 Aug 2005) — the cutoff shifts every cycle
  • Departmental Delhi Police candidates (Constables, Head Constables, ASIs with 3+ years' service): relaxed ceiling of 30–35 years, depending on category
Age RelaxationSC/ST +5 years · OBC +3 years · Ex-servicemen +3 years after deducting military service · widows/divorced/judicially-separated women (Delhi Police SI only) up to age 35 (40 for SC/ST) · departmental Delhi Police candidates up to 30 (UR/EWS), 33 (OBC) or 35 (SC/ST).
QualificationBachelor's degree in any discipline from a recognized university. Selection also requires a Physical Standard Test and Physical Endurance Test: height 170 cm male / 157 cm female (relaxed to 165/162.5 cm for hill-area/ST categories, down to 154 cm for female ST); chest 80→85 cm (unexpanded→expanded) for male, no chest requirement for female; PET — male 100 m in 16 sec, 1.6 km in 6.5 min, long jump 3.65 m, high jump 1.2 m, 16 lb shot put 4.5 m; female 100 m in 18 sec, 800 m in 4 min, long jump 2.7 m, high jump 0.9 m. Eyesight standard is unusually strict — 6/6 (better eye) and 6/9 (worse eye) distant vision WITHOUT any corrective lenses or surgery, plus no knock-knee/flat-foot/varicose-vein/squint — worth independently re-confirming if you wear glasses, since this could disqualify glasses-wearers outright.

Exam Pattern

Computer-Based Test (CBT/online), two papers — Paper I (screening + PET/PST in between) and Paper II — followed by a Medical Test. Recruits for Sub-Inspector in Delhi Police and CAPFs. · Paper I: 2 hours. Paper II: 2 hours.
Paper I: General Intelligence & Reasoning50 questions, part of 200 questions/200 marks total, sectional time-lock of ~30 minutes
Paper I: General Knowledge & General Awareness50 questions, part of the same 200Q/200 marks
Paper I: Quantitative Aptitude50 questions, part of the same 200Q/200 marks
Paper I: English Comprehension50 questions, part of the same 200Q/200 marks
Paper II: English Language & Comprehension200 questions, 200 marks (entire paper is this one subject, testing higher-level English proficiency needed for SI report-writing duties)
Negative Marking-0.25 (1/4 mark) per wrong answer in both Paper I and Paper II.
Passing MarksN/A — merit-based ranking, no fixed qualifying %. PET/PST (conducted between Paper I and Paper II) is qualifying (pass/fail on physical standards), not a marks-based cutoff.

Secondary-sourced from multiple coaching-site aggregators, which agree on the Paper I/Paper II structure (200Q/200 marks each, -0.25 marking) for the 2026 cycle; official SSC CPO notification PDF not directly fetched. Fee figure independently confirmed by 2 sources.

Career Progression

Not covered in the official notification — a commonly cited, unconfirmed coaching-site estimate.

  • Sub-Inspector → Inspector — commonly reported around 8–10+ years (by seniority / departmental exam, varies by force)
  • Higher ranks via Limited Departmental Competitive Exams
  • Separately, a pay-level upgrade (MACP) applies after 10, 20 and 30 years of service

How to Apply

Apply on the official site ↗
  1. Complete One-Time Registration (OTR) on the new ssc.gov.in portal (an old ssc.nic.in OTR will not carry over) or via the official "mySSC" mobile app
  2. Opt in to Aadhaar-Based Authentication if possible, to avoid strict photo/signature rejection rules and carrying printed ID to the exam
  3. Log in and fill the SI application — Bachelor's-degree qualification, and (male candidates) whether you hold a valid LMV driving licence, which gates Delhi Police eligibility
  4. Choose up to three exam centres in order of preference
  5. Sit for the live in-application webcam photo capture when prompted (no photo upload option exists)
  6. Upload your scanned signature (6.0 cm × 2.0 cm, JPEG/JPG, 10–20 KB)
  7. Pay the ₹100 fee online — waived for women, SC, ST and Ex-servicemen — by the fee deadline
  8. Use the 3-day correction window right after closing to fix errors (₹200 first correction, ₹500 second)
  9. Download your Admission Certificate and sit Paper-I (General Intelligence, GK, Quantitative Aptitude, English — 2 hours, sectional 30-minute timers per part)
  10. If shortlisted, appear for PST/PET at a CAPF/Delhi-Police-notified centre, then Paper-II (English Language & Comprehension), and finally the Detailed Medical Examination

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does Paper II test only English, when Paper I already has an English section?

SSC CPO recruits for Sub-Inspector roles that involve report writing and documentation, so Paper II is a deeper, dedicated test of English comprehension and language proficiency beyond the general-aptitude English tested in Paper I.

When does the Physical Efficiency Test happen relative to the written exams?

PET/PST is conducted after Paper I (which is used to shortlist candidates) and before Paper II; you must clear the physical standards to be considered for Paper II and the subsequent Medical Test.

Is the negative marking the same across both papers?

Yes — both Paper I and Paper II deduct 0.25 marks per wrong answer, unlike some other SSC exams where negative marking differs by paper.

Do all posts under SSC CPO (Delhi Police SI vs CAPF SI) follow the same exam pattern?

Yes — the written exam pattern (Paper I, Paper II) is common to both Delhi Police and CAPF Sub-Inspector posts; differences arise mainly in physical standards and the specific force/cadre allocation after selection.
