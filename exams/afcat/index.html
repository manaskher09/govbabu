Posts & Pay

Flying Officer (on commissioning, 7th CPC Defence Matrix) · ₹56,100 – ₹1,77,500 (Level 10) + Military Service Pay ₹15,500/monthFlying Branch and Ground Duty (Technical & Non-Technical) officers, Indian Air Force

Flight Cadets receive a fixed stipend of ₹56,100/month during the one-year training period at the Air Force Academy. On commissioning, additional allowances apply (Flying, Technical, Transport, Risk & Hardship, etc.) depending on branch and posting.

Eligibility

AgeFlying Branch (AFCAT/NCC Special Entry): 20–24 years as on 01 Jul 2027 (born 02 Jul 2003–01 Jul 2007); relaxable to 26 years for valid CPL holders. Ground Duty (Technical & Non-Technical, incl. GATE Score Entry): 20–26 years as on 01 Jul 2027 (born 02 Jul 2001–01 Jul 2007).
Age RelaxationNo caste-based age relaxation categories (this is a Defence Group A Gazetted Officer entry); relaxation is only for CPL holders (Flying Branch, up to 26 yrs) as noted above. Candidates must be unmarried until commissioning.
QualificationFlying Branch: Maths & Physics at 10+2 plus a 3-year graduation degree (any discipline) or 4-year BE/B.Tech, or AMIE/Aeronautical Society equivalent. Ground Duty (Technical): 10+2 with Physics & Maths plus a relevant 4-year engineering/technology degree (specific streams for AE(L)/AE(M)). Ground Duty (Non-Technical): graduation in any discipline (specific streams — Admin, Logistics, Accounts, Education (PG), Meteorology — carry their own qualification requirements per the notification.

Exam Pattern

Online CBT (single written test — AFCAT), followed by AFSB (Air Force Selection Board) Interview for candidates who qualify, then medical examination. Technical branch candidates additionally take EKT (Engineering Knowledge Test) alongside AFCAT. · AFCAT: 2 hours. EKT (technical entries only): 45 minutes (separate paper, same test day).
AFCAT100 questions/300 marks, 2 hours — General Awareness, Verbal Ability in English, Numerical Ability, Reasoning & Military Aptitude
EKT (Technical entry only)50 questions/100 marks, 45 minutes — Mechanical/Electrical/Computer Science/Electronics & Telecommunication (branch-specific)
AFSB InterviewQualifying, multi-day (typically 5 days across 2 stages) — Officer Intelligence Rating (OIR) tests, Picture Perception & Description Test, Psychological tests, Group Testing, Personal Interview
Negative Marking+3 marks per correct answer, -1 mark per wrong answer (in both AFCAT and EKT); no deduction for unattempted questions.
Passing MarksN/A — merit-based ranking for AFCAT/EKT written score; AFSB Interview is a separate qualifying (pass/fail) stage, not a fixed percentage, and final merit combines written score with AFSB assessment.

Figures (100Q/300 marks/2hrs, +3/-1 marking, Rs 250 fee, NCC fee exemption) agree across multiple secondary sources (secondary sources, high consistency, high confidence).

How to Apply

Apply on the official site ↗
  1. Register and apply online at afcat.edcil.co.in; Aadhaar Card is mandatory for registration.
  2. Fill in personal, educational and branch-preference details carefully — details cannot be edited after the "Make Payment" step (except during the designated edit window, 29–30 Jun 2026 for this cycle).
  3. Upload scanned photograph (100–200KB JPEG), signature (80–150KB JPEG) and thumb impression (50–100KB JPEG) as specified.
  4. Pay the non-refundable application fee of ₹550 + 18% GST (not applicable for NCC Special Entry/GATE Score Entry) via net banking/card/UPI.
  5. Print and retain the submitted application form as proof.
  6. Appear for the AFCAT written exam (held 08 Aug 2026 for this cycle) at the allotted centre.
  7. Qualifiers are called for AFSB testing (3 stages over ~5–6 days: Stage-I screening — Officer Intelligence Rating + Picture Perception & Discussion Test; Stage-II — Psychological test, Group Tests and Interview) at one of five AFSB centres (Dehradun, Mysuru, Gandhinagar, Varanasi, Guwahati), followed by medical examination.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to take the EKT if I'm applying for a non-technical (Flying or Ground Duty) branch?

No, EKT is only for candidates applying to the Technical branch; Flying and Ground Duty (Non-Technical) branch applicants only take the main AFCAT paper.

Is AFCAT conducted once or twice a year?

Twice a year — AFCAT 1 (around February) and AFCAT 2 (around August/September), each with its own separate application cycle and cutoffs.

What happens if I clear AFCAT but fail the AFSB interview?

You do not get selected for that cycle — AFSB clearance is mandatory regardless of how high your AFCAT score is, and you would need to reapply and clear AFCAT again in a future cycle to get another AFSB chance.

Is there a fixed AFCAT passing percentage I should target?

No official fixed percentage is published — the cutoff is decided each cycle based on vacancies, branch, and category, and normalized against that session's performance, so treat any percentage figure you see online as an estimate, not a guarantee.
